🧠 Behavioral Nudge Theory in Energy Conservation

In traditional energy management, consumers only receive feedback once a month via an electricity bill. By the time the bill arrives, the opportunity to alter wasteful behavior has passed. EcoSense applies Nudge Theory (grounded in Nobel Prize-winning behavioral economics by Thaler & Sunstein) to provide contextual, immediate, actionable green reminders at the exact moment energy is being squandered.

❌ The Unconscious Habit Loop

  • Leaving AC running at 18°C under blankets.
  • Leaving lights & ceiling fans ON when exiting rooms.
  • Leaving chargers, TV set-top boxes in standby mode 24/7.
  • Running energy-intensive appliances during high-carbon peak hours.
  • No real-time awareness of coal burned or carbon emitted.

✅ The EcoSense Green Nudge Intervention

  • Automated prompt: "BEE recommends 24°C: Saves 30% power without sacrificing comfort."
  • PIR sensor simulation detects empty room for >5 mins & alerts user.
  • Standby vampire load detector flags continuous idle wattage.
  • Peak grid alerts encourage load-shifting to off-peak hours.
  • One-click remediation directly in the alert banner.

🔬 Environmental Footprint Mathematical Modeling

To substantiate an Environmental Studies project, energy conservation must be quantified using peer-reviewed greenhouse gas (GHG) accounting standards and grid emission factors.

1. Carbon Dioxide Equivalent ($CO_2e$) Emission Formula

Emissions (kg CO₂) = Electricity Consumed (kWh) × Grid Carbon Intensity Factor (EF)
Where EF = 0.82 kg CO₂/kWh (Average national grid emission factor for fossil-heavy electricity)

2. Thermal Comfort Energy Relation (AC 24°C Standard)

ΔEnergy (%) = (24°C - T_set) × 6.0%
Operating at 18°C instead of 24°C: (24 - 18) × 6% = 36% Excess Power Consumption!

3. Tree Sequestration Equivalence

Trees Needed = Annual CO₂ Emissions (kg) ÷ 21.0 kg CO₂/tree/year
A single standard 1.5-ton AC running 8 hrs/day produces ~2,800 kg CO₂ annually, requiring ~133 trees to offset.
