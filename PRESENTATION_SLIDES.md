### SLIDE 2: THE CLIMATE PROBLEM & THE RESIDENTIAL SECTOR
* **Key Points:**
  - Residential buildings account for **28% of global energy-related greenhouse gas emissions**.
  - Over **70% of electricity** in developing grids originates from thermal power plants burning pulverized coal.
  - Average grid emission factor: **0.82 kg CO₂ per kWh**.
  - **The Dilemma:** People care about the environment in surveys, but waste electricity daily due to habit and lack of feedback.
> **Speaker Notes:**  
> "When we discuss climate change, we often picture distant coal power plants or factory chimneys. But what commands those power plants to burn fuel? It's our wall switches. Every kilowatt-hour we consume releases approximately 0.82 kilograms of carbon dioxide into our atmosphere. Unfortunately, because electricity is invisible and billing arrives weeks later, consumers remain psychologically detached from the environmental damage of their daily habits."

### SLIDE 3: PROBLEM STATEMENT: THE HABITUAL WASTE VECTOR
* **Key Points:**
  - **The 18°C AC Trap:** Running air conditioners at freezing temperatures beneath heavy blankets.
  - **Empty Room Waste:** Fans and lights spinning in unoccupied rooms for hours.
  - **The Silent Polluter (Vampire / Phantom Load):** TVs, consoles, and chargers in standby drawing 5–10% of household power 24/7.
  - **Thermal Leaks:** Cooling rooms with open windows.
> **Speaker Notes:**  
> "Our investigation revealed four major habitual waste vectors. First, the widespread tendency to set ACs to 18°C. Second, leaving lights and fans running when stepping out of a room. Third, phantom power—where appliances in standby mode silently bleed energy around the clock. And fourth, thermal envelope breaches, like running an AC while a bedroom window is cracked open."

### SLIDE 5: THE SCIENCE OF NUDGE THEORY & THERMAL COMFORT
* **Key Points:**
  - **Richard Thaler's Nudge Theory (Nobel Prize in Economics):** Subtle, non-restrictive cues shift human habits more effectively than mandates.
  - **The 24°C National Rule (BEE & ASHRAE):**
    - 24°C is the optimal human comfort standard in tropical climates.
    - Each 1°C below 24°C forces the compressor to consume **6% more electricity**.
    - Running at 18°C wastes **36% more energy**.
> **Speaker Notes:**  
> "Rather than enforcing strict automated blackouts, EcoSense uses Nudge Theory. When a resident sets the AC to 18°C, the system issues a reminder explaining that raising it to 24°C saves 36% power without sacrificing thermal comfort. We empower the user to make conscious, eco-friendly decisions."

### SLIDE 6: THE 6 CORE REMINDER RULES IN ECOSENSE
* **Table / Visual Graphic:**
  1. **Vacant Room Drain:** Alerts when room is empty > 5 mins with fixtures on.
  2. **AC Sub-Optimal Setpoint:** Nudges thermostat from <24°C up to 24°C.
  3. **Thermal Envelope Breach:** Detects open window while AC compressor runs.
  4. **Vampire Load Alarm:** Flags idle standby draw exceeding 20 Watts.
  5. **Geyser Overrun:** Warns when water heater exceeds 20 continuous minutes.
  6. **Peak Demand Stress:** Advises deferring heavy cycles during 6 PM–10 PM peak grid stress.
> **Speaker Notes:**  
> "Here are the six environmental rules embedded in the EcoSense engine. Each rule addresses a specific thermodynamic or behavioral flaw, accompanied by an instant single-click button that allows the resident to fix the issue in one second."

### SLIDE 8: MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS FOR CARBON ACCOUNTING
* **Formulas:**
  - $\text{Carbon Footprint (kg CO}_2\text{e)} = \text{Energy (kWh)} \times 0.82\text{ kg/kWh}$
  - $\text{Coal Combustion Avoided} = \text{Energy Saved (kWh)} \times 0.65\text{ kg Coal/kWh}$
  - $\text{Cooling Water Conserved} = \text{Energy Saved (kWh)} \times 2.40\text{ Liters/kWh}$
  - $\text{Tree Offsets Required} = \frac{\text{Annual CO}_2\text{ (kg)}}{21.0\text{ kg/tree/year}}$
> **Speaker Notes:**  
> "To ensure rigorous scientific grounding, we incorporated peer-reviewed greenhouse gas accounting equations. A standard grid emission factor of 0.82 kg of CO₂ per kilowatt-hour is applied. Furthermore, thermal power plants consume over 2.4 liters of water for cooling per unit generated. By saving electricity, we simultaneously conserve water and prevent coal extraction."
